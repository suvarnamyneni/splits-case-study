02 — Problem and business context

Problem and business context

eXp agents close transactions in SkySlope, but getting paid depends on correctly declaring how the commission is split: the listing commission, bonuses and concessions, earnest money, transaction coordinator fees, referrals, and the split between every agent on the deal. That information becomes a Disbursement Authorization (DA) that goes to the title company, and only then do settlement cheques and payouts follow.

Splits guides you through commission split details and transmits the information to the transaction in eXp Enterprise to be validated by a transaction settlement specialist. You can revisit splits any time before closing to revise information.

  • The earlier flow, labelled “Old Transaction flow (with upload doc)”, made agents upload documents, then fill a splits form, then review the DA, as three separate tabs.
  • One early version sent agents away to a separate form: “You will be redirected to a secure form to help us know more about the sales info, agents involved and agents splits associated with the transaction.”
  • Solo agents with no splits still had to go through the same flow. The team’s notes say: “No splits required, Skyslope remains only for settlement cheque & statement.”
  • Agents needed to hear about DA and settlement events even when they had not opened the app: “Need push notifications… From notification the agent should be able to download the DA.”

Why this work mattered

A wrong split does not stay on the form. The DA is what the title company pays against, and a transaction settlement specialist still validates it in eXp Enterprise. In the earlier design that check happened after the agent had left the page: upload in one tab, the splits form in another, DA review in a third. An even earlier version sent the agent out to a separate form. A missed bonus, referral, or coordinator fee came back as a change request, and the payout waited on that loop.

The pressure was different for each agent, which is why one path could not serve both. A team lead has to declare a percentage for every agent on the deal. A solo agent has no one to split with and was still walked through the same tabs. SkySlope stayed the system of record, so the agent started in one product and had to finish in another. Agents who were not in My eXp missed the DA and settlement events entirely. This study does not cite a ticket volume or a day count for that delay. The design response is the removal of those handoffs, and the targets further down are the way we proposed to know whether it worked.

The three-tab screen and the stepper that replaced it are shown side by side in Final designs.

05 — Goals and success metrics

Goals and success metrics

These goals come from the design notes and the screens. The table is the measurement plan for the flow. Every number in it is a design target or a projected direction. None of it is a post-launch result.

  1. Let agents finish commission splits inside My eXp in one guided flow, instead of across separate upload, form and review tabs.
  2. Skip splits entirely for solo agents and go straight to sending the DA.
  3. Make the DA easy to verify: a readable fee breakdown, view and download, and Approve or Request change with notes.
  4. Keep agents informed from DA to deposit through in-app and push notifications.
  5. Give the same experience on web and mobile.
Design targets. Baselines describe the earlier three-tab flow. The Target column is projected, not measured after launch.
Metric Baseline Target
Time to complete splits Agents crossed three tabs — upload, splits form, DA review — and an early version sent them to a separate form. Target: reduce time to complete splits by ~40%, projected from one guided stepper with Back and Next instead of three tabs.
DA change-request rate Corrections happened after the DA was sent, in a back-and-forth with the settlement specialist, because the fee breakdown was hard to check in context. Target: fewer change requests per transaction. Projected direction: errors caught on the form, and the ones that remain sent back with notes instead of a restarted flow.
Splits submitted without settlement-specialist rework Specialists re-validated splits the agent could not verify: empty exception fields, team percentages, and fees that did not say what the default was. Target: first-pass acceptance becomes the common case for solo deals and for team deals left on the defaults. Projected, and only countable once review events exist.
Time from close to payout Payout waited on a correct DA. Agents who were not in the app heard about a problem late, or not in the product at all. Target: a shorter close-to-deposit path by cutting avoidable correction cycles. Projected direction only — this study does not claim a number of days.
Agent adoption of the mobile app Splits, Send DA, and approval were a desktop tab flow, so an agent away from a desk could not finish or answer a DA. Target: the same tasks completable on mobile. Projected success is parity — solo Send DA, the four-step form, and a DA action from a notification — not an install count.

Proposed measurement, not a tool already credited with results: product analytics in the Heap or Mixpanel style. Events would be splits started, each step completed, Save & Complete, solo Send DA, DA approved, and change requested, with platform and solo-versus-team as properties. Until those events exist, the targets above stay targets.

06 — Process and iterations

Process and iterations

Design started September 13, 2023. Phase 1 was discovery: personas, paper sketches, and mobile wireframes. Phase 2 and Phase 3 were stakeholder review rounds, first on the transaction module and then across the app. They closed into the current flow for web and mobile. The phases are named in the file; an exact launch date is not.

  1. Personas

    Max, Julian and Alex, each with needs, challenges and scenarios.

  2. Sketches and wireframes

    Paper sketches, then mobile wireframes: three home options, home variants by lifecycle (before active, under 30 days, over 30 days), learnings, cart and checkout, notifications, profile, my team, team member detail, revenue share, my payments.

  3. Design: mobile and web

    Splash and onboarding, day-one and week-by-week home screens per persona, invite, learnings, tools, my payments, team leads, my leads, notifications; web home and my mentees.

  4. Phase 2: Transaction module

    Old transaction flow with document upload; Transaction flow 2.A with document upload and autofill, a staff-side document review, a Chrome extension uploader; then the new Splits form, DA approval cases and settlement notifications; a simpler mobile version and a no-splits flow for solo agents.

  5. Phase 2: All modules (web)

    Dashboard, my payments, paycycle calendar, wallet, profile, rev share details, invoices, LUCY joining bot, team invites, documents, learnings, affiliated services, ICON production home cards.

  6. Phase 3 (web and mobile)

    New joining and sign-up, ICA signing, sponsor questions, payment details, profile per role, My Wallet (renamed from Payment method / bank account), team invites, influencer group rev share reports.

  7. Current app flow design

    Web and mobile flows brought together: consolidated navigation, YTD Income (earnings), documents, wallet, notifications, transactions and the new splits form.

Feedback that shaped later iterations

  • Set up the design system to eXp’s design guidelines for both mobile and web.
  • Make the header sticky on all web pages, create a Tabs component, add a percentage to progress bars.
  • Cut the left nav to 12 items: Home (Tools), My trainings, My transactions, My earnings, My wallet, My documents, My team, Reports, Notifications, Affiliated services, Edit profile, Get help.
  • Add a “Do you have a sponsor?” question, a sponsor detail page and a team version of the join bot.
  • My commission: add referrals to “Amount received”, a filter for unpaid on web, and a link to the paycycle calendar.
  • On web, offer a download link for the iOS and Play Store apps.

What the review rounds surfaced

Synthesized from the Phase 2 and Phase 3 notes. These are review insights, not quotations and not a usability-test transcript.

  • The three-tab page was the flow reviewers were ready to leave. Splits and the DA had to stay on one screen, the header had to stick, and tabs had to be a real component, including a percentage on progress.
  • Yes/No questions were acceptable only when No removed the inputs. Empty fields left on the page read as unfinished work.
  • Solo agents were a separate case. The working note was that no splits are required, and SkySlope remains the place for the settlement cheque and statement.
  • Navigation had spread past what an agent in a transaction could scan. A later round cut the left nav to twelve items and asked for iOS and Play Store links on web.
  • DA and settlement events had to reach an agent who was not in the file, and the notification had to open the DA rather than drop them on Home.
  • Design-system alignment to eXp’s guidelines was still an open to-do, and the mobile transaction module was marked as a new version in progress. Those stay open threads.

08 — Key design decisions

Key design decisions

  1. 01

    Two tabs instead of three

    The old Upload documents / Splits Form / DA Review tabs became Commission Splits and DA Review on one Transaction Details page, so splits and their result stay side by side.

  2. 02

    A three-step stepper for a dense form

    Sales Info, then TC & Referral, then Splits break dozens of fields into chunks the agent can finish one at a time, with Back and Next Step on each.

  3. 03

    Yes/No questions that reveal their inputs

    “Are there any bonuses/additions?” and similar questions show their inputs only on Yes. The team’s note: “yes has input, no removes input”.

  4. 04

    Defaults the agent only edits by exception

    Bonuses and concessions split the same as commission, and each agent pays their own capping fee and eXp commission by default. Review and risk-management fees follow the commission split unless the agent says otherwise.

  5. 05

    One split table for the whole team

    Each agent’s commission, bonus, concession, capping fee and eXp commission sit in one sortable table with Add Agents, which suits team leads like Max.

  6. 06

    A persistent transaction summary

    Address, status, a Buyer/Seller toggle, listing sales price, cap amount left and scheduled close date stay pinned above every step.

  7. 07

    A short path for solo agents

    “Are you a solo agent on this transaction?” Yes goes straight to Send DA, as the team’s notes asked.

  8. 08

    A DA review agents can verify

    The commission fee breakdown collapses, the PDF can be viewed or downloaded, and there are two clear outcomes: Approve, or Request change with notes. Status tags (Change Requested, Approved) and View History follow.

  9. 09

    Tagged notifications

    Settlement and DA events are tagged Action Required or Information and link back to SkySlope where the work lives.

  10. 10

    Meet agents in SkySlope

    A “Manage Splits” card on SkySlope’s Commission tab links to My eXp, so agents start splits from the tool they already use.

09 — Final designs walkthrough

Final designs walkthrough

Before and after

Upload documents, Splits Form, and DA Review were three tabs. The new Transaction Details page keeps Commission Splits and DA Review together, and walks the splits form as Sales Info, TC & Referral, and Splits.

Entry from SkySlope

The SkySlope Commission tab shows the transaction details plus a “Manage Splits” card that explains splits and links to My eXp. A later variant adds a closing-document checklist (closing settlement statement, copies of agent and eXp checks, pay at closing or by direct deposit) with Attach and Unaccept actions.

Step 1: Sales Info

The agent enters the listing commission (amount or %), says whether it is a personal sale, adds bonuses and concessions (amount or %, description, type), and says whether eXp holds the earnest money, giving its amount and date.

Step 2: TC & Referral

The agent says whether a transaction coordinator is paid, whether they are an eXp agent, and whether they should appear on the DA, then gives their name and email. Referral details follow (name, email, brokerage, agent, %), with a prompt to upload the W2 and referral agreement to SkySlope. Last come any bonuses or concessions on the referral and who the primary eXp agent is.

Step 3: Splits

The per-agent table sets commission, bonus, concession, capping fee and eXp commission. Yes/No overrides cover the transaction review fee and risk management fee, followed by the TC fee assignment and any fees between agents. Save & Complete ends the step.

DA review and approval

“DA sent to Title Company” shows the DA as a PDF card with the full commission fee breakdown. The approval state reads “Please review the DA for correctness…” and offers Request change with notes. Once approved: “Your pending transaction is visible under my payment. Once the closing completes, we will let you know when the money is in your bank.”

Mobile

The mobile version has the commission splits screen with the solo-agent question and Send DA, the four-step splits form, DA sent to title, and a notifications list covering settlement documents verified, statement needs correction, and DA sent to title.

11 — Outcomes and impact

Outcomes and impact

What the work delivered is a build-ready splits and DA flow for web and mobile, carried through Phase 2 and Phase 3 into the current app flow. That is a design outcome. It is not a launch report, and it is not an adoption number.

What the design delivered

  • A three-step stepper — Sales Info, TC & Referral, Splits — in place of the upload, form, and DA tabs, with the transaction summary pinned on every step.
  • A solo-agent skip: “Are you a solo agent on this transaction?” goes straight to Send DA.
  • Create-transaction with the purchase agreement attached and the deal fields filled from that document, so agents are not retyping data they already keep in SkySlope. Staff still get a document-review queue, and the Chrome-extension path keeps a document uploader.
  • DA review an agent can actually check: fee breakdown, view and download, Approve, or Request change with notes, plus status tags for Change Requested and Approved.
  • Mobile parity for the same jobs: the solo question, a four-step form, and notifications tagged Action Required or Information that link back to SkySlope.
  • An entry where the work already was. SkySlope’s Commission tab carries a Manage Splits card into My eXp, and a later variant adds the closing-document checklist.

How the review rounds landed

Phase 2 and Phase 3 were the sign-off path. The later screens show what those rounds changed: a sticky header, a tabs component, twelve navigation items, the sponsor question, referrals on amount received, and a link to the paycycle calendar. The question “which of these three tabs do I finish?” is gone from the current flow. Settlement specialists still validate the DA. The design gives them a cleaner document and gives the agent a way to send a correction with notes instead of starting the file over.

Reduced ambiguity is the qualitative result I will stand behind. I am not reporting minutes saved, a change-request rate, or a faster payout. Those remain the targets in Goals until the events are in product analytics.

12 — Learnings and next steps

Learnings and next steps

What worked

  • Personas shaped role-specific home, team and earnings views
  • Defaults and Yes/No disclosure kept a heavy financial form manageable
  • Designing web and mobile together kept the flows consistent

Open threads in the file

  • “New version (WIP)” of the mobile Transaction module
  • “POST Feedbacks WIP” on the web transaction page
  • Design system alignment to eXp guidelines listed as a to-do

What I learned

I led the splits path, and the exceptions taught me more than the happy path did.

  • Max and Alex needed different forms. A team lead needs one table for every agent on the deal. A solo agent needs that table to disappear. Putting both personas next to the old three-tab flow made the fork obvious, and I would separate them even earlier next time.
  • Defaults did more work than layout. Bonuses that follow the commission split, and fees the agent pays unless they say otherwise, removed a class of blank fields. The rule we wrote down — yes shows the input, no removes it — was the difference between a form people finish and a form they abandon halfway down.
  • I had to design the specialist’s read, not only the agent’s entry. A fee breakdown they can collapse, a PDF they can download, and a change request that carries notes is the whole correction loop. Without it we would have shipped a neater version of the same back-and-forth.
  • Drawing web and mobile in the same round kept the phone form from becoming a second product. When a review asked for something on web, the mobile frame had to answer it before the round closed.

What I would do next

  • Usability-test the stepper with team leads and with solo agents, on the cases that actually break a split: a referral, a transaction coordinator, earnest money held by eXp, and a bonus that does not follow the commission split.
  • Instrument the flow before treating any target as met. Heap or Mixpanel-style events for splits started, each step completed, Save & Complete, solo Send DA, DA approved, and change requested, with platform and solo-versus-team on the event. That is how the ~40% time target, and the change-request target, get confirmed or retired.
  • Close the open threads the file already names: the mobile transaction module marked “New version (WIP)”, “POST Feedbacks WIP” on the web transaction page, and design-system alignment to eXp’s guidelines for both platforms.
  • Watch the first settlement cycle with operations. Specialists still validate the DA. If change requests cluster on one field — referral percentage, the TC fee, the capping fee — that field is the next design problem, not a reason to add a fourth step.
